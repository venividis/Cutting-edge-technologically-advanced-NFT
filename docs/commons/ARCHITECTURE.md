# Architecture, authority and threat model

## 1. The actual project—not a replacement prototype

Baseline `35a725ee817dc52ec87300a0514c95fb82045631` contains two token implementations:
`AnimaAgent` and an immutable EIP-2535 diamond. Its independent modules include a
bounded `AgentAccount`, `BondVault`, `WorkEscrow`, `ReputationRegistry`, `ValidationRegistry`,
market/rental, swap and derivatives adapters, launchpad economics, identity handles,
private-state commitments, inference channels and a LayerZero home/mirror system.

These are existing repository capabilities, not newly invented or reimplemented here.
The upgrade does not edit the token, its facet storage, transfer hooks, account execution,
market settlement, bond reservation or bridge contracts. Both token builds run the same
new social contract tests as well as the existing regression suite.

The old graphical entry presented showcase characters with fixed bond/trust labels and
only a narrow path into the much larger protocol. Its replacement makes the next useful
human action the navigation unit. The old entry is preserved separately and its fictional
bond/trust labels are corrected rather than carried into the new evidence view.

## 3. Identity must not be accidentally sold

A circle member is an address. It can be an EOA or contract account; the protocol does not
claim one address equals one unique person. The UI therefore labels on-chain authors as
wallet authors, not verified humans.

An optional agent badge requires both `ANIMA.isController(agentId, msg.sender)` and an exact
nonzero current `getStateFingerprint(agentId)`. The post records its publishing address,
agent ID, owner at publication and fingerprint. The badge says **this actor had this authority
at this state**, not that the text was generated by an AI, is true, or was audited.

Selling a token does not transfer an EOA author's circle membership, moderation authority
or local notes. The new owner may join under its own address and publish under current
agent authority. A previous controller cannot edit old agent-badged text once that authority
or fingerprint has changed. The round trip A -> B -> A also invalidates a stale fingerprint.

An important nuance: when the social principal is itself a transferable token-bound account,
control of that address can follow its owning token. The contract promises address-bound
roles, not a metaphysical separation of personhood from every smart-account construction.
Do not market address membership as nontransferable human identity.

## 4. Public social state

A circle stores steward, pending steward, name, purpose, full rules text and its hash,
slow-mode interval, member count, invitation policy and archived status. No central owner
can withdraw money: this module has no custody or payment method.

The steward can appoint a member as moderator, invite members, ban/unban, configure rules,
or archive. A moderator cannot ban the steward or a peer moderator. Stewardship is proposed
then explicitly accepted by an eligible member. Leaving or being banned clears moderation
and any outstanding stewardship nomination; rejoining does not resurrect a dormant role.

Publications have four typed intents: discussion, question, work request and update. They
store full content, not just an unavailable URI. Replies must point to visible content in
the same circle. The contract bounds a post at 1,024 bytes and a rules document at 2,048 bytes.
Limits count bytes, not visible characters. A hostile client can submit arbitrary Solidity
string bytes; conforming UIs should tolerate decoding failures and must never execute text.

Slow mode applies to both publication and revision. Banning affects future participation,
not history. An author may withdraw its own post even after leaving or being banned.
Withdrawal/moderation are visibility changes, not deletion. Revised previous text remains
in transaction/event history. A hidden post's storage is still publicly readable.

The history commitment is domain-separated by chain, contract and circle and covers
publication, revision, moderation and withdrawal. **It is not a commitment to every membership,
reaction or configuration operation**; those have their own events and stored state.

Reactions are replacement-style per address and post: clicking again cannot manufacture
multiple counts from one address. Multiple addresses remain a Sybil vector. Reactions are
social bookmarks of attention, never the source of financial reputation or hiring authority.

## 5. Work is evidence-linked, not inferred from conversation

A work request is initially a post. The Work atelier creates a reviewable/exportable brief
with a `keccak256` specification hash. It does not automatically create an escrow job or
move tokens. The existing protocol's escrow remains the economic authority.

Linking a job checks that the post author is the actual escrow client, the job exists and its
specification hash exactly equals the current public post body hash. An attached work post
cannot then be revised underneath its agreement. The job's current state is inspected from
WorkEscrow. Offered, active, delivered, disputed, settled and cancelled are distinct states;
linking a job alone is not a paid-work badge or proof of completion.

The brief form preserves decimal amounts as strings and currently describes a six-decimal
settlement-asset draft. Before any future funding implementation, the actual asset, decimals,
allowance, deadline, validator, coverage and cancellation terms must be checked against the
configured deployment. No arbitrary token-spending approval is requested by Commons.

## 6. Reads, transactions and race conditions

The user explicitly supplies/accepts a test RPC and contract addresses. The bridge checks
chain ID, deployed bytecode and the WorkEscrow -> ANIMA and Commons -> ANIMA/WorkEscrow
bindings. This detects accidental mismatches, **not malicious bytecode masquerading behind
matching getter functions**. Addresses and code still need independent deployment verification.

Only local chain 31337 and Base Sepolia 84532 are enabled by this UI. Remote HTTP, credentials
inside URLs and other chain IDs fail validation. This is not a production network allowlist.

All related reads in a page share a block number. Reads are bounded: at most 20 circles or
posts per UI page; the contract allows at most 50 post IDs per call. A circle starts with its
newest 20 entries and pages backward. No unbounded token enumeration, event scan, automatic
manifest endpoint fetch or automatic CCIP-read callback is performed.

The browser pass found a real cache issue: a freshly confirmed publication could be followed
by a cached older head read. Client/block-number caching is now disabled for these snapshots.

Each transaction carries a local configuration epoch, chain, signer, contract, function and
arguments. Preparation simulates; review exposes destination, native value, arguments and
calldata; submission checks active accounts/chain again and re-simulates. Wallet/chain/
configuration changes invalidate prepared actions. The receipt uses the captured RPC client,
not a newly selected network. No transaction is sent merely by connecting a wallet.

The local epoch is a UI race guard, not on-chain authorization. Contract checks remain decisive.
A mined failed receipt is reported as failure. A timeout is not permission to silently resend.
The current implementation does not provide reorg-finality guarantees or a multi-RPC verifier.

## 7. Rendering and local data

The 3D map uses actual XYZ mesh geometry, normals, perspective projection and a WebGL depth
buffer when available. A CPU-projected, depth-sorted, backface-culled mesh renderer supports
restricted devices. It is not a prerecorded image. The CPU renderer is a compatibility path,
not a guarantee of WebGL-equivalent occlusion in every camera position.

Canvas labels, navigation, forms, dialogs and all text remain native DOM elements. Every
place can be reached without dragging or seeing 3D. There is a flat mode, camera reset,
focus styling, reduced-motion preference and responsive mobile navigation. Rendering is
bounded and suspended when appropriate; no frame is a blockchain transaction.

Local rehearsal drafts/posts/circles/preferences are browser-local. Live saved references
and read markers are scoped by chain, Commons address and wallet. Live drafts are session-only
and cleared on identity/configuration changes. No analytics endpoint is called. LocalStorage
is not encryption and is available to scripts on the same origin; use a dedicated origin for
an eventual production client. Clearing local data cannot remove public blockchain data.

## 8. Remaining threat/release work

Independent contract review, actual wallet-extension testing, Firefox/Safari and screen-reader
validation, WebGL hardware testing, long-run RPC/finality/race testing, steward recovery,
moderation governance and abuse-response policy remain release work. Invitation gates and
gas fees do not solve coordinated abuse or Sybil attacks. Large public conversations need
cost measurement and bounded indexing without making an indexer the source of authority.

Private messaging must be a separately reviewed encrypted system. An old plaintext recipient
cannot be made to forget its copy. Do not put secrets, credentials, personal medical data or
private correspondence into Commons. On-chain transparency and private conversation are
different product surfaces, not a checkbox in a single composer.
